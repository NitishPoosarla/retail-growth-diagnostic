# MEMO TEMPLATE — follows the pyramid principle

> Copy this structure for every client memo. No memo ships without all five blocks.
> **Golden rules:** every % carries its denominator; significance ≠ importance;
> data-quality problems are stated before any conclusion.

---

## [Memo title] — [one-line subtitle]
**To:** [Client stakeholder]  **From:** [Analyst]  **Date:** [YYYY-MM-DD]
**Decision this supports:** [the one decision the reader must make]

---

### 1. Answer first (1 sentence)
> **[The recommendation or headline, in plain business language. If we cannot answer
> it, say so explicitly and say what is missing.]**

*Example: "The ad campaign lifts conversion reliably, but we cannot recommend scaling
spend until revenue-per-conversion is supplied."*

---

### 2. Three supporting findings (each with numbers)
**Finding 1 — [short label].**
[Statistic] = [value], on a base of **[n]** ([denominator]). [Interpretation.]

**Finding 2 — [short label].**
[Statistic] = [value], on a base of **[n]** ([denominator]). [Interpretation.]

**Finding 3 — [short label].**
[Statistic] = [value], on a base of **[n]** ([denominator]). [Interpretation.]

> Every finding: point estimate **+** uncertainty (CI or p-value) **+** the business
> translation (e.g. "≈ +X extra conversions per 10,000 users").

---

### 3. So-what recommendation
- **Do now:** [action]
- **Decide after [missing input]:** [action]
- **Do not do:** [action / trap to avoid]

---

### 4. Data-quality & limitations (must appear before conclusions are trusted)
- [Issue] → [effect on credibility: high / medium / low] → [mitigation]

---

### 5. Appendix pointer
- Method & formulas: `deliverables/outputs/...`
- Segment tables: `deliverables/outputs/...`
- Data dictionary: `scripts/...`
